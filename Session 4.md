**Environmental Urgency** The sky darkens unnaturally fast. Clouds gather overhead. A cold wind—_wrong_ for the season—whips across the outcrop. Any PC with Survival (DC 12) or common sense realizes:

- **A ritual is drawing power from the land itself.**
- [[Oakbarrow]] is the epicentre.
- There are _minutes_, not hours.


_The wind cuts sharp and cold as your party races toward Oakbarrow, the unnatural darkness pressing down like a living thing. The path winds through tangled scrub and ancient stone markers—some weathered smooth by centuries, others carved fresh with symbols that make your skin crawl._

_Roll Perception checks, all of you._

---

## **The Encounter: The Shepherd's Warning**

**DC 10:** You spot movement ahead on the path, a figure stumbling toward you, arms windmilling.

**DC 12:** It's a _shepherd_, half-wild with terror, his clothes torn and stained with something dark. Behind him, the ground seems to _shimmer_ oddly, as if heat-warped, though the air is bitterly cold.

**DC 14+:** You notice something worse: his footprints in the dirt _glow faintly_ with a sickly pale light, and they lead _backward_ toward Oakbarrow, not away from it.

---

## **The Scene Unfolds**

The shepherd, Gareth, lurches toward you, gasping. His eyes are wild, unfocused. When he speaks, his voice cracks:

> _"Don't... don't go that way. The stones are **singing**. They're calling him back. My brother—he went to look at the lights and now he **won't stop walking**. Won't stop walking toward the barrow. I tried to stop him, but the wind—the wind just **pushes** you that direction, and the stones, they're **glowing**—"_

He grabs the nearest PC by the arm, his grip surprisingly strong.

> _"They took my brother. The masked ones. They made him drink something from a cup, and now his eyes are **wrong**. Pale. Like milk. He just keeps walking toward the light, and I can't—I can't make him stop—"_

---

## **The Complication: The Entranced Shepherd**

As Gareth speaks, another figure emerges from the mist behind him.

**This is Gareth's brother, Aldwyn.**

Aldwyn walks with an _unnatural_ gait—smooth, measured, almost dreamlike. His eyes are indeed pale, milky-white, and his lips move silently, as if reciting words only he can hear. He wears the same torn shepherd's garb as his brother, and his bare feet leave those faintly glowing footprints.

**Gareth's voice rises to a wail:**

> _"Aldwyn! Aldwyn, please! Come back! The barrow can't have you!"_

But Aldwyn does not acknowledge his brother. He simply _walks past the party_, slowly but inexorably, heading toward Oakbarrow.

**If the party tries to stop him:**

- _Gentle restraint (non-magical, non-violent):_ Aldwyn is docile, pliant, like moving a sleepwalker. But the moment you release him or try to turn him around, he _gently, with eerie calm, continues toward the barrow_. It's as if an invisible rope pulls him forward.
    
- _Magical compulsion (Dispel Magic, Remove Curse, etc.):_
    
    - **Dispel Magic DC 14** reveals a faint aura of _enchantment_ wrapped around Aldwyn's mind. Success breaks the compulsion temporarily—he snaps awake, confused and terrified, gasping: _"What... where am I? I remember the cup, and then the stones were singing, and I couldn't stop my legs..."_
    - But the compulsion is _strong_. Without ongoing magical intervention, it reasserts itself within 1d4 rounds.

---

## **The Skill Checks & Information**

**Insight DC 12** (reading Gareth):

- Gareth is genuinely terrified and telling the truth as he knows it. He's also nearly hysterical with guilt—he couldn't save his brother, and he's wracked with it.

**Medicine DC 13** (examining Aldwyn):

- His pulse is slow, almost too slow for a living man. His skin is cold, clammy. The glowing footprints fade after a few minutes but reappear as he walks—as if the ritual's mark refreshes with each step.

**Arcana or Religion DC 13** (the glow, the compulsion):

- This is _ritual magic_, old and potent. Someone has marked Aldwyn as a _vessel_ or _anchor_ for the ritual occurring at Oakbarrow. His presence there will _feed_ the working—whether as a sacrifice, a focus, or a living conduit.

**Survival DC 12** (the path, the weather):

- The unnaturally cold wind blows _directly toward the barrow_. Walking against it is possible but exhausting (costs an extra 10 feet of movement per round of travel). Walking _with_ it is dreamlike, easy—which is likely why Aldwyn walks so peacefully.

---

## **The Moral Knot: What Do You Do?**

The party now faces a choice:

**Option A: Save Aldwyn, Delay the Ritual**

- If they can cure him and _keep_ him away from Oakbarrow, the ritual loses a focus. But Dispel Magic only lasts a round or so; they'd need to get him to safety (back toward Greenhollow, perhaps) or find a way to _sever_ his connection permanently.
- _Cost:_ Time. Every round spent here is a round closer to the ritual's completion.

**Option B: Let Aldwyn Walk, Race Ahead**

- The hard choice. Gareth will _not_ accept this easily—he'll beg, plead, maybe even try to physically stop the party. But if they push on, they might reach Oakbarrow before the ritual's climax.
- _Cost:_ Gareth's trust, and the knowledge that they've abandoned a dying man to a dark fate.

**Option C: Split the Party**

- One or two PCs stay with Aldwyn and attempt to cure him; the others race to Oakbarrow.
- _Cost:_ Divided strength at a crucial moment. The barrow awaits, and the party is weakened.

**Option D: Improvise**

- Perhaps they bind Aldwyn, carry him, or use creative magic (Feather Fall on a rope, anyone?). Reward clever thinking, but make it cost something (spell slots, exhaustion, time).

---

## **Environmental Pressure**

As the encounter plays out, the sky grows _darker still_.

- Distant chanting rises—rhythmic, hypnotic, in a language no one recognizes.
- The standing stones visible on the path ahead begin to _glow_ with that same pale light.
- The wind picks up, now carrying whispers—fragments of voices, half-heard names.

**Any PC with a Survival check of 15+** realizes the ritual is likely reaching a _crescendo_. They have perhaps 10–15 minutes before something irreversible happens.

---

## **Gareth's Role Going Forward**

- If the party is kind to him, he'll offer to take Aldwyn back to Greenhollow himself (if they manage to cure him) or to stand guard at the path's entrance.
- If they ignore or mock him, he'll follow at a distance, bitter and frightened—potentially becoming a liability or a future hook (what if the cult finds him later as a hostage?).
- If they ask, he can tell them: _"The masked ones came at dusk. They had a cup with... with something **silver** in it. They made Aldwyn drink. They said he was 'chosen for the Opening.'"_



**Immediately Ahead (within 100 feet):**

The narrow track rises gently, flanked by gnarled hawthorn and blackthorn bushes—twisted, thorny things that seem to lean _toward_ the path as if watching. The grass underfoot is pale and frost-touched, despite the season being wrong for such cold. Your breath mists in the air.

Scattered along the verge are **standing stones**—weathered markers, some carved with moss-worn symbols you cannot quite make out. They're old. Very old. And they seem to _multiply_ as you progress, as if the land itself is marking a boundary you're crossing into.

**Middle Distance (200–300 feet):**

The path crests a low rise, and beyond it the landscape opens. You can see **Oakbarrow itself**—a modest village of stone cottages clustered atop a gentle, grass-covered mound. But the mound is _wrong_. It rises too perfectly, too symmetrically, and it's ringed by a circle of **standing stones**, each taller than a man, each carved with symbols that make your eyes water when you try to focus on them.

**The Stones Glow.**

A faint, sickly pale light emanates from them—not bright, but _present_, like distant lanterns seen through fog. The light pulses slightly, rhythmic, almost like breathing.

**Closer Detail on the Barrow:**

The village buildings cluster at the base of the mound, dark and silent. No smoke rises from chimneys. No figures move in doorways. It's as if the place has been _abandoned_—or emptied.

Above the village, atop the mound itself, you can see the outline of what might be a cairn or ancient tomb—a structure of stacked stones, partially overgrown with moss and creeping ivy.

**And the Sound:**

The chanting you heard before is now _louder_, no longer distant. It's coming from the barrow itself. Rhythmic, hypnotic, in a language that sounds old—older than words. Male and female voices, perhaps a dozen or more, all speaking in unison:

> _"...aesh...karath...vel-thir...aesh...karath..."_

The words seem to hang in the cold air, and with each repetition, the pale light from the stones _pulses brighter_.

**The Wind:**

It still blows _toward_ the barrow—steady, pushing, as if the land itself is drawing you forward. Walking against it requires effort (extra 10 feet of movement); walking with it is almost dreamlike, effortless.

**The Road:**

The path splits roughly 50 feet ahead. The main track continues straight toward Oakbarrow's eastern approach, passing through a gap in the standing stones. A fainter path branches left, skirting the stones—longer, more exposed, but avoiding the direct approach.

**What You Notice With a Successful Perception Check (DC 13):**

- Fresh footprints in the pale grass, leading _toward_ the barrow. Many of them. Some are bare feet (Aldwyn's glow-prints?), others are shod and deliberate—the masked cultists, perhaps, numbering at least a half-dozen.
- The footprints are _recent_—within the last hour or two.
- Near the fork in the path, a child's cloth doll, stained with dirt and what might be dried tears, lies abandoned. It bears the mark of small hands clutching it in fear.

**The Moral Weight:**

The barrow awaits. Gareth's brother walks that path. The kidnapped parents are somewhere beneath those stones. The ritual is _crescendoing_. And the small child you carry—or lead—looks up at you with wide, frightened eyes and whispers:

> _"That's where they took them. That's where the bad people went. Can you... can you make them bring Mama back?"_
