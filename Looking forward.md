**Immediately Ahead (within 100 feet):**

The narrow track rises gently, flanked by gnarled hawthorn and blackthorn bushes—twisted, thorny things that seem to lean _toward_ the path as if watching. The grass underfoot is pale and frost-touched, despite the season being wrong for such cold. Your breath mists in the air.

Scattered along the verge are **standing stones**—weathered markers, some carved with moss-worn symbols you cannot quite make out. They're old. Very old. And they seem to _multiply_ as you progress, as if the land itself is marking a boundary you're crossing into.

**Middle Distance (200–300 feet):**

The path crests a low rise, and beyond it the landscape opens. You can see **Oakbarrow itself**—a modest village of stone cottages clustered atop a gentle, grass-covered mound. But the mound is _wrong_. It rises too perfectly, too symmetrically, and it's ringed by a circle of **standing stones**, each taller than a man, each carved with symbols that make your eyes water when you try to focus on them.

**The Stones Glow.**

A faint, sickly pale light emanates from them—not bright, but _present_, like distant lanterns seen through fog. The light pulses slightly, rhythmic, almost like breathing.

**Closer Detail on the Barrow:**

The village buildings cluster at the base of the mound, dark and silent. No smoke rises from chimneys. No figures move in doorways. It's as if the place has been _abandoned_—or emptied.

Above the village, atop the mound itself, you can see the outline of what might be a cairn or ancient tomb—a structure of stacked stones, partially overgrown with moss and creeping ivy.

**And the Sound:**

The chanting you heard before is now _louder_, no longer distant. It's coming from the barrow itself. Rhythmic, hypnotic, in a language that sounds old—older than words. Male and female voices, perhaps a dozen or more, all speaking in unison:

> _"...aesh...karath...vel-thir...aesh...karath..."_

The words seem to hang in the cold air, and with each repetition, the pale light from the stones _pulses brighter_.

**The Wind:**

It still blows _toward_ the barrow—steady, pushing, as if the land itself is drawing you forward. Walking against it requires effort (extra 10 feet of movement); walking with it is almost dreamlike, effortless.

**The Road:**

The path splits roughly 50 feet ahead. The main track continues straight toward Oakbarrow's eastern approach, passing through a gap in the standing stones. A fainter path branches left, skirting the stones—longer, more exposed, but avoiding the direct approach.

**What You Notice With a Successful Perception Check (DC 13):**

- Fresh footprints in the pale grass, leading _toward_ the barrow. Many of them. Some are bare feet (Aldwyn's glow-prints?), others are shod and deliberate—the masked cultists, perhaps, numbering at least a half-dozen.
- The footprints are _recent_—within the last hour or two.
- Near the fork in the path, a child's cloth doll, stained with dirt and what might be dried tears, lies abandoned. It bears the mark of small hands clutching it in fear.

**The Moral Weight:**

The barrow awaits. Gareth's brother walks that path. The kidnapped parents are somewhere beneath those stones. The ritual is _crescendoing_. And the small child you carry—or lead—looks up at you with wide, frightened eyes and whispers:

> _"That's where they took them. That's where the bad people went. Can you... can you make them bring Mama back?"_

## **What the Party Senses (Survival DC 12):**

The ritual is reaching a _critical point_. You have perhaps **10 minutes—no more**—before whatever is being summoned or awakened becomes irreversible. The pale light grows slightly brighter with each chanted phrase.
