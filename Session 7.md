**The Road to the Styx**

The party does not reach the River Styx in peace. Avernus has no mercy for the unguarded, and the road between the pits and the blackwater crossing is patrolled by more than beasts. The party is ambushed by a band of demonic scavengers: ash-skinned raiders with rusted chain, infernal sigils carved into their armor, and eyes full of blood-red hunger.

These are not soldiers of a formal warlord. They are killers, looters, and smugglers, the sort who survive in Hell by preying on the desperate. Their target is simple: either take Lyra, or take the Palantier.

> "The pale glass sings when the girl breathes," one of the raiders hisses. "The ledger wants it. The masters want the girl. We just want the price."

### The Ambush

The party sees the road narrow between jagged black stone on one side and a smoke-choked ravine on the other. A wave of demonic bandits erupts from the rocks, hoarse and brutal, snarling as they rush in with hooked chains, jagged spears, and a desperate, unnatural frenzy.

The leader is not large, but he is cunning. He focuses on Lyra if she is visible and tries to seize her with a chain or drag her away from the party; if Lyra is not exposed, he targets the Palantier, trying to wrench it from the holder's hands or break the party's formation long enough to steal the artifact.

### What They Want

These bandits are low-level opportunists but dangerous in numbers. They are not trying to kill the party outright; they are trying to escape with a hostage or a relic. If they manage to grab Lyra or the Palantier, they attempt to flee toward the darker stretches of the road, where the ferryman's shore is not far away.

### Encounter Tactics

- The raiders strike hard and fast, trying to isolate one target.
- The leader uses intimidation and threats, not just blades.
- If one bandit gets a clear path to Lyra or the artifact, the rest of the group tries to cover his escape.
- They are careful to avoid a lethal fight unless it becomes necessary; they prefer a quick take and run.

### Spotting the Ambush

- Perception DC 13: A character notices the dust shifting too early and the shadows moving wrong.
- Insight DC 14: A character reads the raiders' posture as prearranged and predatory rather than random.
- Investigation DC 13: A character notices the stained chain and infernal marks that hint at a bargain with a stronger power.

### If the Party Defeats Them

The surviving raiders are stripped of any obvious wealth, but one of them carries a token: a black lacquered coin stamped with a stylized branch and a ledger-mark. More importantly, they are carrying a scrap of parchment with a single phrase written in infernal script:

> "The ferryman takes debt, not blood. The crossing is open to the living for the right price."

This is the first clear sign that the River Styx is not just a hazard, but the route home.

### If the Party Captures One

A captured raider can be questioned with Intimidation, Persuasion, or a simple threat of pain. They confess a rough truth they have heard from others in the pits:

- The Styx is the road to the material world
- The ferryman is a being of old law and debt
- He will carry those who can pay, or who can offer him something he values
- He does not care who you are, only what you owe

This knowledge is crucial: the ferryman is not a random monster, but a gatekeeper who can take them back to Alderwyn if they know how to bargain.

## **The Approach**

As your party reaches the obsidian shore, the River Styx stretches before you like a wound in reality—its waters are not water, but liquid shadow, viscous and cold, emanating a palpable sense of dread. The air is heavy with old sorrow and the smell of old graves.

A lone ferryman awaits at a weathered dock, his bark-grey hands resting on a pole of blackened bone. His form is difficult to focus on. When you try to look directly at him, your eyes slip away and settle somewhere just beyond him. He is not cruel, but there is something in him that feels older than mercy and more absolute than law.

The bandits and their attempted theft are a reminder that the road is not safe, but the ferryman is the true obstacle between Avernus and the rest of the world.

---

## **The Ferryman's Offer**

The ferryman does not speak like a demon and does not speak like a man. He speaks like a ledger being read aloud by thunder.

> "Payment. One soul coin per passage. Place them upon the water, and your crossing is assured."

But the party does not need to take his words at face value. If they are careful, they can discover more.

### Reading the Ferryman

A character can make a Wisdom (Insight), Intelligence (Religion), or Intelligence (History) check to understand what the ferryman really is and whether he can take them back to Alderwyn.

- DC 12: They realize he is not a random beast or devil but a ferryman of the dead, bound to the River Styx.
- DC 15: They recognize the ferryman as a lawful, ancient being connected to the flow of souls, debts, and passage between realms.
- DC 17: They realize he is not just a toll collector—he can carry the living to the far side if the price is paid or the debt is accepted.

On success, the party learns this:

- He is bound to the River Styx and takes souls and obligations alike.
- A living person can cross if they pay, or if the ferryman accepts a debt.
- He can carry them back to Alderwyn, but only if the price is sufficient and the bargain is accepted.

### Bargaining for Passage

The ferryman is not interested in coin alone. He is interested in obligation, and in the exposure of the living to old law.

A character may attempt a Charisma (Persuasion) or Intelligence (Insight) check to negotiate a reduced price or to identify an acceptable form of payment.

- DC 14: The ferryman accepts a bargain tied to a debt, service, or favor rather than raw coin.
- DC 17: The character sees exactly what he values—memory, a vow, a promise, or the right to claim a future favor.

Possible bargains include:

- One soul coin and a favor owed to the ferryman
- A promise that the party will return to the river under his terms one day
- A small relic or magical item of value
- A vow spoken aloud before the boat departs

Failure means the ferryman takes his price in a harsher way. The party still crosses, but at a cost.

## **The Toll & The Danger**

**The Transaction**

_The ferryman's voice emerges like wind through a crypt:_

> _"Payment. One soul coin per passage. Place them upon the water, and your crossing is assured."_

**The Temptation of His Gaze**

Here is where your players face the true peril. As they approach to pay, the ferryman will naturally meet their eyes—or attempt to. This is no accident. The ferryman's gaze holds the weight of eternity in it.

**Intelligence Saving Throw** (DC 15):

- **Success:** Your character avoids his gaze in time, turning their head slightly or shielding their eyes. You feel the brush of something vast and terrible, but it does not take hold. Cost: 1 soul coin per passenger, or an agreed bargain.
- **Failure:** _Your eyes lock with his._

---

## **Curse of the Ferryman's Gaze**

When a character fails the save, do not rush to explain. Describe it first:

> _Your eyes meet his, and for a moment—just a breath—you see everything. Every soul he's ferried. Every debt unpaid. Every curse whispered over these waters. It is too much. The weight of eternity presses against your mind. You feel something ancient and angry notice you._

**Choose ONE of these curses to inflict** (or let the player choose, if merciful):

1. **The Bargain**: The ferryman grants passage, but the cursed character now "owes" him a favor—one he will collect at a dramatically inconvenient moment. Until then, they suffer disadvantage on saves against being charmed or frightened.

2. **The Weight**: The character's soul feels heavier. They suffer disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws (from water, traps, etc.) as the Styx's pull grows stronger. "The river remembers you."

3. **The Haunting**: A shade—a fragment of a soul the ferryman once carried—attaches itself to the character. Once per day, they must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC 15) or be frightened for 1 minute as it whispers accusations and regrets in their mind.

4. **The Debt**: Most dramatically—the character's name is now written in the ferryman's ledger. He collects payment not in coin, but in years of life. The character ages 1 year (cosmetic, unless you want it to be permanent). The ferryman is now their creditor, and they will feel his influence calling to them when they venture into danger.

---

## **The Crossing Itself**

_Once tolls are paid_ (and curses, if any, are incurred):

The ferryman poles his craft—a vessel of rotted wood and shadow—across the river. The journey is not long, but it feels eternal. Time moves strangely here. Your players may experience:

- Whispers from the water—voices of the damned calling their names
- Glimpses of faces pressing against the surface, as if trapped beneath
- The ferryman's silence, which is somehow worse than speech
- A sensation that the far shore is both impossibly close and infinitely distant

**Arrival:** Your party reaches the far bank. Behind you, the ferryman poles his craft back toward the near shore without a word. The curse of his gaze—if any were afflicted—settles into their bones like winter ice.

---

## **DM's Notes for Play**

- Make the ferryman feel alien. He is not evil—he is beyond morality. He is duty incarnate.
- Describe the intelligence save carefully. A player might ask, "Can't I just look away?" Yes, but the ferryman expects payment first. To avoid his gaze is to invite suspicion—or worse, an automatic curse for refusing the toll.
- Use the curse as a plot hook. The ferryman's favor, the weight upon them, the haunting shade—these are seeds for future complications. A cursed character is now marked by Avernus itself.
- Don't soften the consequences. Avernus is ruthless. A player who fails the save should feel that failure ripple forward.

## **What Comes Next?**

Once they cross, the true nature of their Avernus journey begins. The Blood War rages. Devils scheme. And your party—marked by the ferryman's gaze, if they were careless—carries a piece of the Hells within them.

_Where do they venture next, brave adventurers? Toward the Bleeding Tankard for intelligence? Into the war machines graveyard? Or do they have another destination in mind?_

---

## **Session Summary**

This portion of the Avernus arc is the transition from desperate flight to deliberate escape. The roadband ambush shows the party they are being hunted, and the ferryman gives them the means to leave the plane—if they can survive the bargain and the gaze. This is the first real moment where they understand that the Legion of Avernus is not the only force moving in the world: the River Styx itself is a gate with laws and debts, and the debt is not always paid in coin.
